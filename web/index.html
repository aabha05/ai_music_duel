EmbervsTide

Two AI musicians take turns improvising short tunes, each trying to out-play the other. This walkthrough shows you what they do and how they think, using real duels composed by the project.

Meet the two musicians

Neither of these musicians is a recording. Each one is a small program that writes its own music on the spot, then listens to its rival and answers back, like a rap battle with melodies.

The rules of the duel

  1. They take turns. Each turn is one short tune, 2 bars long.
  2. A judge gives every tune a score out of 10.
  3. A good tune sounds natural, fits the background chords, matches the mood, and works as a reply to the rival’s last tune.
  4. When the turns run out, the higher total wins and plays the ending.

How does a musician choose what to play?

Like a chess player, it thinks a few moves ahead. Here is what Ember did before playing its very first tune.

  1. Invent options. A genetic algorithm breeds hundreds of possible tunes, keeping and mixing the best ones like natural selection, until 5 strong options are left.
  2. Imagine the future. For each option, Ember asks: what’s Tide’s best reply? And then what’s my best answer to that?
  3. Assume the rival is smart. Ember picks the option that still leaves it ahead even when Tide replies perfectly. This is called minimax.
  4. Skip hopeless options early. As soon as Ember sees that Tide has a crushing reply to an option, it stops examining that option. This shortcut is alpha-beta pruning.

The winner plays the ending

Music feels finished when it lands back on its home note. Getting there smoothly, with small steps instead of awkward jumps, is a route-finding problem, just like a map app finding the quickest road. The program solves it with A* search.

Everything that happened, in order

Every step below is a different AI technique from the course, and each lives in its own file.

  1. Fuzzy logic sets the mood

    Vague words like “fairly dark” become exact numbers: tempo, key, how busy the notes are. fuzzy_mood.py

  2. A neural network learns what music sounds like

    It studied 11 famous old tunes against random noise, and now rates any tune from 0 to 1 for how natural it sounds. critic.py

  3. A judge scores every tune out of 10

    It combines the neural network with music rules: harmony, mood and whether the tune works as a reply. judge.py

  4. A genetic algorithm invents options

    It breeds and mutates tunes over many generations, and hill climbing polishes the best ones. genetic.py

  5. Minimax with alpha-beta picks the move

    Each musician looks three turns ahead and skips hopeless branches. adversarial.py

  6. A* search plans the ending

    It finds the smoothest path back to the home note. cadence.py
